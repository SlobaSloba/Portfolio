Data Overview¶

This notebook analyses survey responses from 5,000 mobile developers, covering eight topics: geographic region, organisation size, role, years of experience (mobile and general software), platforms developed for, app categories, programming languages used, and primary target audience. The survey questions were answered in three ways:

  • Single-choice (region, organization size, experience, target audience) - coded as numeric values corresponding to categories (e.g., 1 = Western Europe, 2 = Eastern Europe, etc.);
  • Multiple-choice (app categories, roles, programming languages) - coded as 1 for selecting an option and NaN otherwise;
  • Platform preference - one option marked as "Primary", any number of others as "Also using", and NaN for the rest.

The dataset includes a wide variety of participants across all measured variables, without obvious overrepresentation of any group. Response completeness was high for region (100%), experience (100%), and platforms (99%), but noticeably lower for organisation size (71.9%), roles (67.5%), languages (57.1%), and app categories (43.5%). All analyses on these variables are therefore based on available cases only. To preserve comparability, relative values (%) within available cases are used as the primary statistic.

Notebook structure:

  2. Exploratory analysis - missingness, sample composition
  3. Part B: Platform reach - primary vs. secondary platform targeting and cross-platform combinations
  4. Part B: Android vs. iOS developer profiles - geography, experience, organisation size, roles, categories, and languages

The first step is to import the necessary libraries and load the data. Ordinal variables are recoded based on the codebook so that answers are human-readable, and columns are renamed to intuitive snake_case names for easier coding throughout.

Platform Reach¶

The following section analyses how many developers primarily target each platform, how many use others as secondary, and what the most common primary + secondary combinations are.

In [6]:
# First we iterate through all columns, and then extract the labels by removing the "platform_".
platform_cols = [c for c in df.columns if c.startswith("platform_")]
platform_labels = [c.replace("platform_", "") for c in platform_cols]

# Then we count the number of the two tags in the columns.
platform_summary = pd.DataFrame({
    "Primary":    [df[c].eq("Primary").sum() for c in platform_cols],
    "Also using": [df[c].eq("Also using").sum() for c in platform_cols],
}, index=platform_labels)

# And then define additional columns in the df to get the remaining stats of interest (counts, %)
platform_summary["Total reach"] = platform_summary.sum(axis=1)
platform_summary["Primary %"] = (platform_summary["Primary"]    / len(df) * 100).round(1)
platform_summary["Also using %"] = (platform_summary["Also using"] / len(df) * 100).round(1)
platform_summary["Reach %"] = (platform_summary["Total reach"] / len(df) * 100).round(1)
platform_summary = platform_summary.sort_values("Total reach", ascending=False)

print(platform_summary.to_string())

The remainder of the analysis compares developers who list Android as their primary platform against those who list iOS. The focus is on differences that are notable in magnitude and potentially meaningful to a business reader, rather than exhaustively cataloguing all similarities and differences.

Two groups are defined: Android primary developers and iOS primary developers.

Several meaningful differences emerge from the single-choice comparisons:

Geography and market economics: Platform choice is strongly tied to geographic location. Android development is more prevalent in emerging markets (South Asia, Middle East & Africa, South America), while iOS leads in mature, high-income markets (North America, Western Europe).

Experience and decision-making power: iOS developers are more experienced professionals, both in mobile development and general software development.

Organisation size and target audiences: Android developers are more likely to work alone (14.7% vs 9.2%) and are more commonly uncertain about their target audience (11.1% vs 7.1%). iOS developers are more concentrated in larger enterprises (9.5% vs 6%) and more often target well-defined corporate clients (10% vs 6.9%).

Summary of Key Findings¶

Platform landscape:
Android is the dominant primary development platform (81.5% reach), with iOS and Mobile Browser following. Cross-platform targeting is the norm - most developers, regardless of primary platform, also target at least one other.

Android vs. iOS profiles:

Dimension Android developers iOS developers
Geography Emerging markets (South Asia, MEA, South America) Mature markets (North America, Western Europe)
Experience Less experienced on average More experienced; more senior roles
Organisation size More likely to work solo or in small teams More common in large enterprises
Target audience Consumer apps (Education, Games); often uncertain Enterprise & premium consumer (Finance, Health, Business)
Languages Java, Kotlin Swift, Objective-C

Strategic implication:
Businesses targeting enterprise buyers should prioritise iOS channels. Those targeting emerging markets should prioritise Android. Android developers are better targets for freemium and consultancy-style services; iOS developers are more apt for high-ticket, multi-seat enterprise software licences.
